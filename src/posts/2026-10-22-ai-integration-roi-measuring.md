# AI Integration ROI: How to Measure What Actually Matters

Every executive asks the same question after the AI pilot demo: "What's the actual return?" The honest answer is: most teams don't have a good framework for measuring it, so they make it up.

This article presents a measurement framework that connects AI integration to real business outcomes — not vanity metrics like "time saved per task" that don't move the P&L.

## The Three Tiers of AI ROI

Tier 1 is **direct labor displacement**. A model that replaces 40% of an analyst's report-writing time genuinely saves money. Measure it: hours automated × fully-loaded hourly cost. If your analyst costs €80/hour fully loaded and you save 8 hours/week, that's €33,000/year per analyst. Real, attributable, defensible.

Tier 2 is **throughput improvement**. A model that doesn't replace a person but lets them handle 2x the volume of customer tickets, sales calls, or code reviews. This is harder to measure because the baseline is fuzzy — what *would* have happened without AI? Use A/B testing: split the team, measure outputs, compare. The ROI is the revenue or cost-avoidance from the delta.

Tier 3 is **option value**. AI enables things you couldn't do before — a recommendation engine, a 24/7 support layer, a personalized onboarding flow. These don't have clean ROI because they wouldn't exist without AI. Estimate conservatively: what would you pay for this capability if it were a SaaS product?

## What to Track

Three numbers, updated monthly:

1. **Cost per AI-assisted outcome.** Total AI inference cost + integration maintenance divided by outcomes produced. Should drop over time as you optimize.
2. **Outcome quality variance.** Are AI-assisted outputs as good as human-only? Sample 5% of outputs, score them, track distribution.
3. **Time-to-value for new use cases.** When you identify a new use case, how long from decision to first measurable result? This is your organization's AI velocity.

## What to Ignore

- "Calls to the LLM" (means nothing)
- "User satisfaction" (sample size too small to matter)
- "Coverage" (% of cases where AI is used — irrelevant if the use cases are low-value)

## The Hardest Question

The hardest question isn't "does AI work?" It's "for which 3-5 workflows is AI genuinely the right tool, and which should stay human-only?" Most companies over-invest in AI for workflows where it adds cost without value.

The answer comes from the framework: measure tier 1 and tier 2 outcomes for each candidate workflow. The top 3-5 by ROI get the investment. Everything else waits.

## Closing

AI ROI isn't a single number. It's a portfolio decision. The companies that get this right are the ones that treat AI integration like any other capital investment: measured, prioritized, and ruthlessly cut when it doesn't perform.
