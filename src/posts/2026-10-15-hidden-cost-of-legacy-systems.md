Most companies don't know what their legacy systems really cost. The license fee is
visible. The downtime is felt. But the slow bleed of developer productivity, security
debt, and missed opportunity is invisible—until it isn't.

## The three buckets of legacy cost

**1. Operational drag.** A 15-year-old ASP.NET WebForms application might "work,"
but every change takes weeks instead of days. New business rules require database
schema surgery, hand-rolled state management, and manual regression testing. Your
most senior engineers spend their time decoding tribal knowledge instead of building
new features.

**2. Talent tax.** Developers don't want to maintain COBOL, classic ASP, or Windows
Forms. Recruitment cycles stretch, contractor rates climb, and the institutional
knowledge concentrates in two or three people who are approaching retirement. When
they leave, the cost isn't a recruitment budget—it's an existential risk.

**3. Opportunity cost.** The CRM your sales team complains about is also the CRM
that can't integrate with your AI-powered forecasting tool. The inventory system that
"works fine" is the same one that blocks real-time pricing. Every quarter on the
old stack is a quarter your competitors move ahead.

## How to quantify it

We use a 4-axis audit with every prospective client:

- **Time-to-change:** How many engineering hours to ship a typical new feature?
- **Incident frequency:** Mean time between failures, severity-weighted.
- **Integration ceiling:** How many modern APIs/tools can plug in without custom
  middleware?
- **Security debt:** Outdated dependencies, unsupported runtimes, missing patches.

Most audits surface $200K–$2M/year in hidden cost for a mid-market company.

## What modernization actually delivers

A successful modernization program doesn't just "make it new." It compresses
time-to-change by 5–10x, unlocks modern tooling (AI, real-time analytics, mobile),
and turns your engineering org from a maintenance crew into a product team.

The companies that invest now will compound the advantage for the next decade. The
ones that wait will pay the eventual ransom—either to a rescue team during a
catastrophic failure, or to a competitor who moved faster.

If you're not sure where you stand, start with the audit. The data will tell you
what your gut already knows.
