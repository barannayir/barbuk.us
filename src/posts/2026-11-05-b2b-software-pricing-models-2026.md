# B2B Software Pricing Models That Work in 2026

The B2B software pricing world has fundamentally shifted. Per-seat is dying. Usage-based is rising. And the companies that haven't figured this out are leaving money on the table — or worse, leaving customers frustrated.

This is a practical guide to choosing the right pricing model based on your product, your customers, and your go-to-market motion.

## The Death of Per-Seat

Per-seat made sense when software was a tool used by individuals. In 2026, the value comes from workflows, integrations, and automation — not from individual logins. Charging per user for an automation platform is like charging per passenger for an airplane: it punishes the customer for using the product successfully.

Three signals your per-seat model is broken:
- Customers are buying fewer seats than they need (usage throttling)
- Land-and-expand isn't working (customers consolidate instead of growing)
- Your biggest competitors are using usage-based or outcome-based pricing

## The Five Models That Actually Work

**1. Tiered subscription with usage overages.** A predictable base + a variable component tied to value. Works for SaaS with mixed usage patterns. The base covers support and platform costs; the overage captures growth.

**2. Pure usage-based.** Pricing per API call, per transaction, per GB processed. Best when customer value is highly variable and outcomes are measurable. Examples: Twilio, Snowflake, AWS.

**3. Outcome-based.** Pricing per result delivered — per qualified lead, per resolved ticket, per closed deal. Hardest to execute, highest ceiling. Examples: a few pioneering AI companies.

**4. Platform fee + consumption.** A fixed platform access fee plus variable consumption. Works for products with a clear platform-vs-usage split. Examples: HubSpot, Datadog.

**5. Tiered by user role.** Different prices for different roles (admin, contributor, viewer). Better than per-seat because it prices by who actually drives value, not by who's in the org chart.

## How to Choose

Ask three questions:

1. **Where does the customer see value?** Per-transaction? Per-user? Per-outcome? Price where value is felt.
2. **How variable is usage?** If usage is steady, subscription works. If it varies 10× month-to-month, usage-based is fairer.
3. **Can you measure outcomes?** If yes, outcome-based is the highest-ceiling model. If no, fall back to usage.

## What to Avoid

- **Per-seat for AI/automation products.** Punishes customers for adopting the product.
- **Pure outcome-based without good measurement.** Leads to disputes and customer trust issues.
- **Too many tiers.** Three tiers max. Five tiers is decision paralysis.

## The Migration Path

If you're on per-seat and want to transition, do it in two phases:

**Phase 1:** Add usage-based add-on (e.g., per API call, per GB). Keep per-seat as base. Watch the data.

**Phase 2:** Once you understand the usage distribution, redesign tiers around value. Move customers to new tiers with grandfathering.

Most companies that try to switch overnight lose 20-40% of revenue in the transition. The two-phase approach cuts that to 5-10%.

## Closing

Pricing is product strategy. The right model aligns the vendor and customer around the same outcome: the customer succeeds, the vendor gets paid for that success. In 2026, the companies that get this right will outpace the ones still charging for seats.
